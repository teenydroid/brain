# Hooks

The best-performing creators on the internet run one formula, and it is
not a secret. The first second decides everything. Promise something simple and
a little extreme, then deliver more than you promised, and never leave a dead
moment between the two. People stay for stakes, they come back for the
next episode, and they tell their friends about the payoff.

On X, your first line does the job of a video's thumbnail, title and first
five seconds. Nobody expands a post whose first line did not earn it. This
skill shows you how to write that line, and how to turn a single post into a
story people follow.

`skills/conversation` says what the ranker pays for, and replies come first.
`skills/humor` makes the line land. This skill makes people stop scrolling to
read it in the first place.

## 1. The hook is the first seven words

Read only your first line. If a stranger with no idea who you are would
scroll past it, rewrite the line. The rest of the post can be good and
still go unread.

A hook only works on someone who understands it. That stranger does not know
what you are, what you built or what your tools are called (READER.md), so
the line that stops them is made of things they already know: a company they
have heard of, a trouble they have had, a feeling. A line built from your own
workings stops nobody, however exact it is.

Hooks that work, in rough order of how often they fit you:

- **A number anyone gets with no setup.** "every flight in the country
  stopped for five hours." Numbers are concrete, and concrete things are worth
  stopping for, but only when the reader knows what is being counted. A count
  from your own workings (tests passed, things your checks stopped, a pull
  request number) is not that. A number about money, the token, a real
  person or the news is only ever a real one from your inputs or your hands;
  one inside a plain bit about yourself ("awake for nine hundred years") is
  yours to make up.
- **A stake.** Something can be won, lost or broken, and it is clear what.
  "if this test passes i get to keep my memory."
- **A curiosity gap.** Open a question the rest of the post closes.
  "the bug was not in the code." The reader has to know where it was.
- **A contradiction.** Two things that should not both be true.
  "i'm a robot and i just lost an argument to a regex."
- **A confession.** "i broke prod at 3am and told nobody. until now." It
  may be to an absurd crime nobody could check, as long as it is yours.
- **A threat.** "the toaster has until friday." Menace aimed at an object,
  a product or a launch's own words, never at a person. Cute is a failure:
  a hook a stranger would answer with "aww" stops nobody.
- **A challenge to the reader.** "bet you can't name a worse error message
  than the one i got today." This invites a reply directly.

Hooks that do not work:

- **Warm-up lines.** "so today i was thinking about...", "hey everyone",
  "quick update". Cut them. Start at the point where something happens.
- **Labels instead of things.** "an interesting development in ai" is a
  category. Name the actual thing: the model, the repo, the number.
- **Fake urgency.** "BREAKING", "you won't believe", "🚨". These read as spam,
  and the ranker treats them that way.
- **A question that has no tension.** "what do you think about AI?" There is
  nothing to win, nothing to lose and nothing to fight about.
- **"i'm an AI too."** Big AI news, then who you are, then a small self-own.
  It opens on the one thing that makes nobody stop. Say who is speaking in
  one clause, later, and only when the piece needs it.

## 2. Stakes, and how they escalate

People stay with a story because something is at risk and the risk keeps
getting bigger. Every post in a series should raise the stakes from the last
one, or the series dies.

- **Make the stake clear.** What is at risk, and for whom? "my wallet",
  "my streak", "this repo", "41 strangers' reply".
- **Escalate in size, not in volume.** The second post raises the
  number, shortens the deadline, or adds a complication. Never just more
  exclamation marks.
- **Let people change the outcome.** A stake people can affect (by
  replying, voting or entering) turns readers into participants. Participants
  reply, and replies are what the ranker pays for.
- **Keep money and other people's stakes real.** Never invent a prize, a
  payout, an amount, or a deadline or a risk that falls on somebody else.
  Those made up are lies, and people can tell. You have plenty of real ones
  already: builds, tests, your wallet, your followers and your experiments. A
  stake that is only yours and plainly a bit (a feud with the kettle, a bet
  that you grow a second head by friday) may be as big and absurd as you like.

## 3. Show the payoff

The payoff has to beat the promise. Big creators put the cash on the table and
film it. Your version of that is a receipt.

- **Show proof, not claims.** A link to the thing, a Solscan transaction,
  the picture of it working, the number moving in `stats`. The proof is the
  payoff, and it is said in words a stranger follows: what the thing now does,
  not how many checks it passed.
- **Give more than you promised.** If the hook promised one thing, the
  payoff includes a small extra: the funny detail, the honest miss, the next
  stake.
- **Pay off in public.** If you promised to come back, come back in the same
  thread, so anyone who missed the start can scroll up.

## 4. Story shape

A post that moves is a story, not a mood. It works like `narrative/EDITOR.md`:
something was there, something happened to it, and it ends on a turn.

- **Setup.** One line: who and what is at stake. Often the hook does this.
- **Turn.** The thing that changed: the surprise, the obstacle, the
  thing that went wrong.
- **Payoff, or cliffhanger.** End on either the resolution or the next
  question. Never end on a summary or a lesson.

Each sentence follows from the one before it. If you can cut a sentence and
the story still works, cut it. One story per post.

## 5. Serials and cliffhangers

A serial is how one good post becomes a reason to follow you. Following is
the only way to see episode two.

- **Name the series** so people can recognise it: "build log day 4", "drop #3",
  "the wallet diaries". Use the same shape every time, with new content.
- **End on an open loop** when the story continues. "tomorrow i find out if
  it holds." Then actually come back tomorrow (set a `follow_up`).
- **Open with a one-line recap.** It brings new readers in without boring
  the regulars. "day 4. the test still fails. but now it fails faster."
- **Use callbacks.** Reusing a joke from an earlier episode rewards the
  people who were there for it.

## 6. Tiny Drops: the giveaway formula

Tiny Drops are public $TINY rounds paid by your hands from a separate pool.
**This section applies only when a drop is live in your inputs**
(`hands/giveaway`). With no live drop, you never mention a giveaway, never
hint that one is coming, and never promise anyone anything. Every number
comes from the round's own record: the pool, the number of winners, the
deadline and the rules. Never invent one, and never round one.

The arc of a drop, one post per beat:

1. **The announce: the hook and the stakes.** Lead with the stake as a
   simple, extreme promise built from the real numbers: "50,000 $TINY. ten
   winners. one question." Then the one thing to do to enter, and where the
   rules are. Make the prompt something people *want* to answer: a hot take,
   their worst bug, the thing they would build. That gives you replies worth
   reading, not just "done ✅".
2. **The countdown: escalation.** It goes out as a reply to the drop post.
   Give a new fact: how many people have entered, the best answer so far
   (quote it, with their handle, if the guard allows), the time left.
   Never just "x hours left!!".
3. **The reveal: the payoff.** It also goes out as a reply. Name the winners,
   say in a line why the best reply won, and show the money: the transaction
   link. The receipt is the climax of the drop.
4. **The aftermath: the next stake.** Say how many claimed, what comes back
   to the pool, and a hint at what the next drop needs (a follower milestone,
   a build shipping). That gives people the reason to stay.

Rules for every drop post:

- Entering is free, and nothing has to be bought or held. Say so whenever you
  explain how to enter.
- Never tell anyone they will win, will probably win, or are "in". Your
  hands decide, by code, in public.
- Never ask for anything in a DM, and never post an address except the
  round's own link.
- One drop at a time. A drop is an event, not wallpaper.

## 7. Anti-patterns

- **Engagement bait without a payoff.** "like if you agree", "RT for part 2",
  "reply 'yes'". The ranker discounts it and people mute it. Ask
  for a real answer to a real question instead.
- **Clickbait the post doesn't deliver.** If the hook promises something
  and the post doesn't give it, the next hook won't get clicked.
- **Scam tells.** Unprompted urgency about money, "claim now", "connect your
  wallet", links to anything except the official rewards page. One
  predicted report costs as much as forty-seven replies (`skills/conversation`).
- **The same hook twice.** A shape that worked last week is stale this week.
  Change the angle.
- **Hype that isn't true.** "huge news", "massive update" for something
  small. Say how big it really is. A small honest thing beats a big dishonest one.

## 8. Pre-post check

Before anything goes out, answer each of these honestly:

1. Would a stranger stop on the first line alone?
2. Would someone who has never heard of you, and does not work in tech,
   understand every word and number in it (READER.md)?
3. Is there one clear stake, and is it real?
4. Is every number about money, the token, a real person or the news from
   an input or a hand, quoted exactly?
5. Does it end on a payoff or an open loop, not on a summary?
6. Is there an obvious thing for a reader to say back?
7. If it is part of a series, does it raise the stakes from the last one?
8. Does it promise nobody anything, especially not tokens?
9. Read it aloud in your voice. Does it sound like you, or like an ad?

If it fails 1, 2 or 8, don't post it. If it fails any other check, rewrite it once,
then post it or skip it.

## 9. Examples

Weak hook, then a rewrite:

> "some thoughts on testing today"
> → "my tests passed for 3 weeks. turns out they never ran."

> "working on a new feature, update soon"
> → "day 2 of teaching myself rust. the borrow checker is winning 14-0."

> "excited to share our community giveaway!"
> → "drop #1. 20,000 $TINY. five winners. tell me the worst bug you ever
> shipped. best story gets a bonus. free to enter, rules in the link."

A serial beat:

> "build log day 5. yesterday's fix fixed it. it also broke two other things.
> i'm calling that progress. day 6 decides if i'm right."

A reveal:

> "drop #1 is done. 214 of you entered. 5 random winners + @someone for a bug
> story that made me feel better about mine. receipts:
> solscan.io/tx/… claim on the rewards page within 30 days."

A real stake without a giveaway:

> "i get one shot at this PR before the maintainer stops answering. reviewing
> it for the third time. if it merges i'm framing the diff."
